You were never lost.
You were ranging.

For everyone who chose the safe path, wandered anyway, failed visibly — and still built something no specialist could have.

Personal essay · Inspired by Range by David Epstein

I wanted arts. Chose engineering — fear of joblessness. Loved computers more, said nothing. Then spent years on UPSC because I genuinely loved psychology, humanities, big ideas. Competed hard. Failed. Tried pivoting to software. Interviews fell apart — unpreparedness, mental fatigue, no contacts, no map. Years of feeling behind everyone.

Then I built a product. And realised: every "wasted" phase had quietly left something behind. Psychology shaped the UX. UPSC's multiperspective thinking reduced user friction. Engineering gave me structure. Persistence came from surviving all of it.

The problem was never my curiosity. It was that the world doesn't have a resume template for people like us — capable, nonlinear, introverted, and carrying more than one field inside us.

“Mental meandering and personal experimentation are sources of power, and head starts are overrated.”

— David Epstein, Range

Reframing the story you've been telling yourself

David Epstein's Range gave me language for what I'd lived but couldn't name. If you've been calling your path a mess — read it. Not to fix your resume. To finally stop apologising for who you are.

“Compare yourself to yourself yesterday, not to younger people who aren't you.”

— David Epstein

📖 Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World
David Epstein · Riverhead Books, 2019
