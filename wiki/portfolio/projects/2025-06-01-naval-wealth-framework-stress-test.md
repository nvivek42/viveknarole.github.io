# Naval wealth framework stress test

## Bold Claim

Frameworks are useful only when they survive friction. A wealth model should be stress-tested against reality, not admired in isolation.

## Pattern Observed

The common high-level wealth advice often sounds elegant but becomes fuzzy when applied to actual tradeoffs in income, risk, leverage, and life design. The problem was not the ideas themselves; it was the gap between theory and decision-making under real conditions.

## Action Taken

- mapped the core ideas of the Naval wealth framework into explicit decision principles
- tested them against realistic constraints and scenarios
- looked for where the model created clarity and where it created false certainty
- evaluated how leverage, compounding, opportunity cost, and personal judgment interact

## Result

The framework became more actionable and more honest. It clarified what matters, what is noise, and what requires deeper custom thinking rather than generic advice.

## Lesson

Good frameworks create structure without creating rigidity. The test is whether they help you think better under variation, not whether they look elegant in a summary.

## Verification

The model was validated by comparing the framework against lived decision scenarios where incentives, risk, and time pressure created real tradeoffs.
